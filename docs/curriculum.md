# The curriculum

Thirty lessons in nine units, in an order that is an argument: each unit assumes only
what the ones before it established. A reader who starts at lesson one and stops at
lesson thirty has walked from «why should I care about these questions» to «this is
what becoming a Muslim actually means», with no step skipped and no step taken on
authority the guide has not yet earned.

Every lesson exists twice, as `src/content/lessons/ar/<slug>.md` and
`src/content/lessons/en/<slug>.md`: same slug, same position, each written in its own
language rather than translated word for word. Arabic is the first language of this
site, and the Arabic file is canonical for `unit`, `order`, `minutes` and `emoji`.

## Who the reader is

Someone circling the big questions. Perhaps a Muslim whose certainty has been shaken by
arguments they had no answer to; perhaps someone with no religion who is honestly
asking; perhaps a reader of another faith. The guide assumes **no prior knowledge and no
goodwill toward religion**, only honesty. It never assumes the reader already accepts
the Qur'an as evidence: the early units argue from reason and observation alone, and
the Qur'an enters first as a text making an argument, not yet as scripture. By the time
the guide asks the reader to weigh the Qur'an as revelation, it has given them reasons.

## The tone, which is a rule

The Qur'an argues with atheism in one verse of Surat at-Tur, and it does it with a
question, not an insult. That is the register of this whole site: confident, warm,
respectful of the questioner even while being direct about the question. We show that a
position fails; we never mock the person holding it. Ridicule wins arguments and loses
people, and this site exists for the person.

## Pages that are not lessons

- **الخلاصة / Summary** (`cheatsheet`): the whole argument on one page: the chain
  from «the universe demands a Creator» to «this messenger is genuine», each link one
  paragraph with its lesson linked.
- **عن الدليل / About** (`about`): who this is for, the methodology, the full sources
  policy, and what this site is **not**: a substitute for scholars, or a debate club.
- **المعجم / Glossary**: الفطرة، التوحيد، الشرك، الوحي، النبيّ والرسول، العقيدة،
  الإعجاز، التواتر، الشبهة، السنّة، الصحابة: each in one merciful sentence, per language.
- **راجِع / Review** (`practice`): every lesson's quiz questions in one shuffled bank.

## Content rules (the ones that will be tempting to break)

1. **Never type a Qur'anic verse**: the pinned-corpus pipeline from learn-tajweed is
   inherited whole. Reference by `ref:`; running-prose quotes go in `«…»` and are
   checked character-by-character against the mushaf.
2. **No weak hadith, ever, even if beloved.** Bukhari and Muslim by default; anything
   else must carry a mainstream sahih/hasan grading, linked (sunnah.com / dorar.net).
   A site arguing for the truth cannot cite what it cannot defend. «I was a hidden
   treasure» and its cousins do not appear here.
3. **No weak «scientific miracle» claims.** The إعجاز علمي genre is full of
   overreach, and one refuted claim costs the site every strong argument it made.
   Scientific facts may *illustrate* a verse; the argument never *rests* on a
   contested scientific reading. When in doubt, leave it out.
4. **Never present a contested point as settled** (inherited from learn-tajweed, and
   it matters more here). Evolution and Adam; the dating details of the Rome
   prophecy; attributions of famous reports: where scholarship genuinely differs,
   the lesson says who holds what and links the source.
5. **Respect the other religions while disagreeing.** The preservation lesson cites
   the other scriptures' own textual scholars, not polemics. No mockery of any
   faith or of atheists; the site's argument for its own tone is 16:125.
6. **Both languages are first-class.** English lessons are written in natural English,
   not translated Arabic. Qur'an renderings in English lessons are explanatory
   («meaning:»), clearly labeled, with a link to full translations on quran.com.
7. **Answer the strongest version of every objection.** If the lesson's atheist
   sounds stupid, the lesson has failed, because the reader has heard the strong version.
