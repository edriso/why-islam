## Who is this for?

Three readers:

- **The honest questioner** — of no religion, or another religion — whom the big questions will not leave alone, and who wants an answer that respects their mind.
- **The Muslim whose certainty was shaken** — who heard objections, found no answer at hand, and concluded there was none. This guide was written to show them the answer exists.
- **The one who gets asked** — a parent asked by their child, a friend asked by a colleague — who wants the argument in order before presenting it.

## The method

- **Reason first.** The early units argue from reason and observation alone; the Qur’an is cited *as revelation* only after the case for that has actually been made. No lesson asks you to grant anything that has not yet been argued.
- **The strongest version of every objection.** An objection appears here in the form its holders give it, not in a weakened form that is easy to knock down. A reader who has heard the strong version and finds a strawman here will conclude — rightly — that we could not handle the strong one.
- **Respect costs the argument nothing.** We show that a claim fails; we do not mock the person who holds it. Our style guide on this is one verse: “Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good counsel, and argue with them in the best manner” (16:125).
- **Contested points are named as contested.** Where qualified scholarship genuinely differs — questions around evolution and the creation of Adam, for instance — the lesson names the positions and who holds them, and never presents one view as the consensus.

## The sources

- **The Qur’an:** every verse on this site is copied by machine from the mushaf text (Hafs ʿan ʿAsim, from the Tanzil project, pinned by checksum) — never typed by hand. Even a short inline quotation is checked character by character on every build.
- **Hadith:** only what is in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, or what specialists have graded authentic elsewhere, with the grading cited. No weak narration appears here however popular it is — a site arguing for the truth cannot argue from what does not hold.
- **Tafsir:** the established commentaries — al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Saʿdi, al-Tafsir al-Muyassar — cited where it matters.
- **And no weak “scientific miracles”:** a scientific fact may illustrate a verse, but no argument here rests on a strained scientific reading of one. A single overreaching claim discredits, in the reader’s eyes, every sound argument standing next to it.

## What this site does not claim

- **It is not a substitute for scholars.** It is an ordered doorway. Whatever remains unclear after it belongs with people of knowledge: “Ask the people of knowledge if you do not know” (16:43).
- **It is not a debating arena.** Its goal is that the reader *understands* the argument, not that an opponent is crushed. Whoever reads it to stockpile comebacks has the wrong door.
- **It does not claim faith is finished at the last lesson.** Guidance is asked of the Lord; evidence only opens the door.

## The site itself

Completely free — no account, no ads, no tracking. Your lesson progress is stored on your own device and nowhere else. The project is open source under the 0BSD license: copy it and reuse it with no condition and no permission needed.
